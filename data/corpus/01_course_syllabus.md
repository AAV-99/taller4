# Course syllabus FH1037 — Introduction to Python for Data Science and AI

## General information
- Code: FH1037. Workload: 4 hours per week for 16 weeks.
- Instructor: Edwin Montoya.
- Format: lectures with hands-on Jupyter notebooks and weekly assignments.
- Prerequisites: none. The course starts from scratch, but students are expected to dedicate 6 hours per week to independent work.

## Course objective
Students will be able to solve simple data analysis problems by writing clear, tested and documented Python programs, as a foundation for later courses in data science and artificial intelligence.

## Course units
1. Unit 1 (weeks 1-3): variables, data types, operators, input and output, conditionals (`if`, `elif`, `else`).
2. Unit 2 (weeks 4-5): loops (`for`, `while`, `range`), accumulators and counters.
3. Unit 3 (weeks 6-8): functions (`def`, parameters, `return`), variable scope, docstrings and testing with `assert`.
4. Unit 4 (weeks 9-11): lists, tuples, dictionaries, list comprehensions.
5. Unit 5 (weeks 12-14): introduction to pandas: `Series`, `DataFrame`, reading CSV files, filtering and `groupby`.
6. Unit 6 (weeks 15-16): final project analyzing a real dataset.

## Grading
| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Weekly assignments (7) | 30 % |
| Concept quizzes (4) | 20 % |
| Midterm exam (week 9) | 20 % |
| Final project | 30 % |

Grades range from 0.0 to 5.0 and the passing grade is 3.0. A student with two missing assignments or an assignment average below 3.0 joins the course tutoring program.

## Tutoring policy
- The tutor (human or intelligent assistant) guides students but does not solve the assignments: it offers gradual hints, conceptual corrections and suggestions for the next step.
- Hints are given in three levels: (1) a guiding question and the concept involved, (2) a concrete hint about the error or the missing step, (3) a partial skeleton or pseudocode with blanks that the student completes.
- The complete solution of a graded exercise is never given, even if the student insists.

## Academic honesty
Copying solutions, sharing assignment code or asking an assistant to write the whole assignment is considered academic fraud. Students may consult the official documentation and ask for explanations of concepts. The use of AI assistants must be declared in every submission.
